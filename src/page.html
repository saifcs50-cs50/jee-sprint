Why it's built this way

The plan in brief

today

The syllabus gets 12 weeks: 1 October to Sunday 20 December. After that you have 4½ weeks for a second pass, 8 mocks and revision before Session 1 (22–30 January). Twelve weeks at 3⅓ hours on college days and 8 on Sundays is about 310 hours for 59 chapters, roughly 5 hours each. That's tight, and it works only if you do four things: 30–40 PYQs per chapter on the first pass (the rest come back in the second pass), skim your class notes for chapters your college has already taught, turn every college holiday into a Sunday, and cut from the drop list without guilt when the pace line says you're behind.

Coaching ads promise 99 percentile. If you are starting from close to zero with a long college day, use your own mock trend as the scoreboard. A steady climb from mock to mock is the target. Session 2 in April is a second attempt with 2½ more months behind it.

Five steps, about 5 hours

How to do one chapter

  1. Learn

    1½–2 h · 1 h if college already taught it

    One lecture from one source at 1.5×. Pause and work every solved example yourself. For chapters your college has already taught, skim your class notes instead of a full lecture.

  2. Main notes

    during step 1

    Formulas, reactions and question types. Write what you would forget, not what the teacher said.

  3. PYQs

    about 2 h

    30–40 questions, newest years first. The 2024–26 papers match the current syllabus. Stuck for 6 minutes? Read the solution, mark it, redo it in 3 days. The rest of the usual 100–150 come in the second pass (21 Dec – 3 Jan), starting with Top chapters.

  4. Short notes

    30 min

    One or two pages, no more. This is what you'll revise from in January, so make it complete.

  5. Test

    Sunday

    The Sunday part test covers it. Every wrong answer goes into the mistake log.

Chemistry

  • Inorganic chapters (Periodic, d & f, Coordination, p-Block, Biomolecules): NCERT line by line is the syllabus. JEE lifts sentences straight from it.
  • Organic: one reaction chart per chapter, reagent → product. Revise it on the bus.
  • Chemistry is the fastest subject to score in from zero. Aim to make it your strongest.

Maths

  • Cap lectures at 90 minutes per topic, then solve. Maths is learned by doing problems.
  • Morning Block 1 is where maths problems belong.
  • Calculus is a chain: functions → limits → derivatives → integrals → area → differential equations. Keep that order.

Physics

  • Draw the diagram before touching a formula.
  • One formula sheet per chapter, with units. Reuse any good ones you already have.
  • Modern Physics and Semiconductors are the cheapest marks in the paper. Don't skip them.

The mistake log

One notebook, one line per wrong or skipped question. Re-read it every Sunday. In January it becomes your most valuable revision material.

DateChapterFromLeakThe one-line fix

The five leaks: Silly (knew it, slipped), Memory (forgot a formula or fact), Concept (didn't understand it), Time (solved it, too slowly), Unattempted (never tried).

Your trend

Exam hall: three rounds

  1. Round 1, about 75 min. Go through all 75 questions. Answer only what you're sure of. Start with Chemistry (it's the fastest), then your stronger of Maths and Physics.
  2. Round 2, about 75 min. Questions you know how to start.
  3. Round 3, last 30 min. Check marked answers, then make careful guesses.

When to guess

Every question is +4 / −1, and since 2025 that includes the numericals. 25 per subject (20 MCQ + 5 numerical), all compulsory, 300 marks.

MCQ situationExpected marks
Two options eliminated+1.50
One option eliminated+0.67
Blind guess+0.25
Numerical, guessed≈ −1

So guess an MCQ once you can rule out one option. Never guess a numerical.

Everything else

Life rules until 30 January

  1. Sleep 7 hours, every night. 22:30 to 05:30. Your brain stores the day's learning while you sleep, so cutting sleep deletes the evening you just studied.
  2. Phone outside the room during every block. Instagram: uninstall it until 30 January, or keep it to DMs only. Uninstalling takes ten seconds. JEE reels feel like studying and aren't. This app is your plan now.
  3. Anything that eats evenings is paused until 30 January. Gaming, long YouTube sessions, side projects. They'll still be there after the exam.
  4. One teacher per subject. Pick your lecture source this week and don't switch. Hopping between sources is the most common way late starters lose November.
  5. Make college count twice. If college teaches a chapter this week, swap it into your plan so those hours double up. College tests are free mocks; analyse them like Sunday ones.
  6. Show your parents this timetable. Ask them to protect 19:45–22:15 and Sunday mornings.
  7. Move every day. A 15-minute walk after college, sport on Sunday evening.
  8. Holidays are what make 12 weeks possible. Every college holiday runs the Sunday timetable: about 5 extra hours each. Give them to the week's biggest chapter.
  9. Bad day? Don't borrow from sleep. Push the unfinished chapter into Sunday's first block. Still behind at the end of the week? Cut from the list below, starting at the top.
  10. Sunday 17:30–20:00 is off. This is what makes 16 weeks survivable.

If you fall behind, cut in this order

Unfinished chapters carry over to the top of the app with a Skip button. When you're more than 3 behind, skip in this order, so the 20 December finish holds. Skipped chapters wait for the buffer weeks (21 Dec – 3 Jan).

  1. Ellipse and Hyperbola (keep Parabola)
  2. Trigonometry beyond identities
  3. Physics low set: Solids, Thermal Properties, Experimental Skills
  4. Rotational Motion beyond moment of inertia and rolling
  5. Ionic Equilibrium numericals
  6. p-Block Groups 13–14

After Session 1

  • 31 Jan to boards: board mode. Write full answers, because board marks come from steps. Use your board's question bank and model papers.
  • Why boards matter: NITs and IIITs through JoSAA need 75% in Class 12 (or top 20 percentile of your board). Some state entrance exams also weigh board marks in their ranks, so check yours.
  • After boards: Session 2 in April, plus any state entrance exam you plan to sit. Same routine, same mistake log.
